02 — Work

What I have built

Two case studies and a few shorter pieces of work. Each card states what kind of work it is and which part was mine, so that team work is not mistaken for my own.

  • Case 01 Internal initiative · Deloitte

    From a spreadsheet methodology to a diagnostic tool

    A methodology for assessing the maturity of the finance function lived scattered across spreadsheets. I developed the web application that replaces it: a team can walk through it in a client conversation without losing the rigour of the model behind it.

    Context
    The model assesses finance capabilities and sub-capabilities across three levers —processes, technology and organisation— with maturity levels from 1 to 5, key questions, evidence and associated initiatives.
    Challenge
    Keep that methodological richness without overwhelming the person using it. A spreadsheet holds the information, but you cannot navigate it in front of anyone.
    My contribution
    Full development of the tool: functional interpretation and structuring of the model, user-experience design and the incorporation of the nine domains, with navigation, comparative radar charts, a maturity heatmap, gaps against the target, priorities and initiatives linked to the diagnosis. The methodological content was developed by the team.
    Scope
    From a first domain to a multi-domain architecture of nine finance domains, all built into the tool: financial planning and analysis, controlling, transactional processes and business finance, among others.
    How it was built
    • Short iterations. A first usable version before a complete version on paper; then improvements in robustness, functionality and design before each review.
    • Version control with Git and GitHub, with separate branches for development and publishing, and only stable versions merged into the main branch.
    • Publishing with GitHub Pages, also solving everything around a deployment: caching, version updates and continuity between sessions.
    • Shared persistence with Firebase, to move from data stored in a single browser to scenarios that several people can carry on.
    • Industrialised domain onboarding: a parameterisable data structure that allows a new domain to be added without rebuilding the tool.
    • Functional and edge-case testing before every delivery.
    • Executive presentation and guided demo, already used in client conversations. Because it is an interactive tool, walking through it explains its value far better than a set of screenshots.
    • Maturity assessment
    • Multi-domain architecture

  • Case 02 Client project · Deloitte

    Planning and controlling for an industrial group, moving to Oracle EPM Cloud

    One of Spain's ten largest business groups by revenue ran its planning and controlling on an earlier platform. The project moved those processes to Oracle EPM Cloud without losing the business logic behind each figure.

    Context
    Long-range planning scenarios, budget, forecast and actuals, covering margins, prices, variance analysis, product profitability and financial management of R&D and investments.
    Challenge
    A model like this is not migrated by copying structures. You have to understand what each dimension and each rule represents before rebuilding it, or the new system returns numbers nobody recognises.
    My contribution
    I worked on the functional design of the dimensional model and the calculation rules (around one hundred of them, for capitalisations, depreciation and financial effects under IFRS and tax criteria), on the functional coordination of more than ten integrations and between thirty and forty mappings, and on validating the loaded data.
    Results
    Processing-time reductions of 80% and 90% in two calculation cubes, and an automated depreciation calculation that removed manual effort equivalent to two full-time employees. The redesign of the rules and of the tax calculation process also reduced recurring errors.
    Diagnosis
    When a figure does not reconcile, the job is to trace it: which rule ran, with which period and scenario, which records were rejected and at what point the mapping stopped transforming what it should.
    What we worked with
    • Oracle EPM Cloud and Hyperion Planning, with Essbase as the calculation engine of the model.
    • Integrations with SAP and other business sources through ODI, FDMEE, Data Exchange and EPM Automate.
    • Data and metadata loads, business rules, mappings and review of execution logs to locate the source of a difference.
    • Validation in test environments before every production update.
    • Analytical outputs: Data Forms, Task Lists, Smart View and dashboards for whoever has to decide.
    • Oracle EPM Cloud
    • Hyperion Planning
    • Essbase
    • ODI
    • FDMEE
    • SAP
    • IFRS

06 — Approach

How I work

The same cycle, whether the deliverable is a planning model or a tool. It is short on purpose: hardly anyone can describe what they need until they can see it working.

  • 01 — Understand

    Turn the need into a concrete improvement

    Before configuring or coding anything, understand how an organisation plans, closes and decides. Much of the value lies in finance and systems meaning the same thing when they talk about the same figure.

  • 02 — Build

    Incrementally and under control

    A first usable version before a complete version on paper. It is easier to correct something you can touch than to argue over a requirements document.

  • 03 — Test

    Functionality, readability and edge cases

    A tool that fails in a demo stops being useful even if the model behind it is right. And data that arrives correctly does not always arrive complete.

  • 04 — Share

    Publish, document and gather feedback

    What only one person knows is not a solution, it is a dependency. Documenting and teaching people to use it is part of the delivery, not an extra at the end.
